Why this work matters now

Building-performance policy is changing the work of ownership and operations.

Washington and Seattle are moving large existing buildings from voluntary efficiency toward documented performance and emissions requirements. That creates a growing need for people who can connect policy, utility data, audits, operations, capital planning, and implementation.

Washington State

Clean Buildings Performance Standard

CBPS covers existing buildings over 20,000 square feet through two tiers. Tier 1 buildings must meet energy-management, operations-and-maintenance, benchmarking, reporting, and—in many cases—energy-performance requirements. Tier 2 focuses on benchmarking, energy management, and O&M requirements.

2026–2028Tier 1 deadlines phased by building size

July 1, 2027Tier 2 reporting deadline

City of Seattle

Building Emissions Performance Standard

BEPS applies to existing nonresidential and multifamily buildings over 20,000 square feet. Reporting and verification begin first; initial greenhouse-gas intensity targets arrive between 2031 and 2035, with increasingly stringent targets leading toward net-zero emissions.

Where SBST fits

Training for the work behind compliance

South Seattle College’s Bachelor of Applied Science in Sustainable Building Science Technology prepares graduates to design, manage, and diagnose high-performing, energy-efficient buildings. My coursework and internships build practical capability in energy auditing, utility-rate analysis, benchmarking, facility management, building systems, financial analysis, and communicating recommendations to stakeholders.

This education aligns directly with building-efficiency and compliance-support roles in Washington and Seattle. Some regulated submissions or sign-offs may require a separately recognized Qualified Person or professional credential; the SBST degree builds the technical foundation and applied experience for that career path.
